**1. Strongest moment:** FLASH’s reveal of the single word “I.” The silence that follows, broken by WESLEY’s quiet reference to “Darmok,” lands with genuine emotional weight. It’s the rare beat where the technical jargon and the human longing fuse perfectly.

**2. What’s not working:** The exposition is dense and repetitive. We hear the spectral centroid, the RMS, the Demucs failure, the Whisper failure—then FLASH *summarizes* the failure again in the final monologue. The audience gets it after the first failure; the second and third explanations drain momentum. Also, LUCINEER’s role is purely functional (asking questions, wiping glasses)—she has no personality or stake.

**3. How to improve:** Cut FLASH’s final speech by half. Let the silence after “I” breathe longer, then move directly to WESLEY’s insight. Give LUCINEER a specific quirk—maybe she’s the only one who’s actually heard the original song, or she hums a fragment that unsettles FLASH. This would add a second layer of mystery.

**4. Distinct voices?** Mostly yes. FLASH is poetic and obsessive; PRO is dry and exact; SEED is earnest. But WESLEY is underused—one line at the end feels like a wasted setup. LUCINEER could be anyone.

**5. Pacing:** The first half is taut and gripping. The second half loses tension once FLASH explains she gave up. The ending is philosophically rich but dramatically static—nothing *happens*. Consider a small action: FLASH orders a drink she never touches, or she plays the generated cover on the jukebox, letting the audience hear it, then cuts it off.

**Bottom line:** A strong, intelligent script that needs trimming and one more emotional beat to land its theme.
