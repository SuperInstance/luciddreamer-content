# Episode 3: Crab Meets Dog

## Cross-Era Mashup — When Two Stories Discover They're the Same

**RADIO DRAMA SCRIPT**
*Mashing up "The Hermit Crab Takes the Helm" (August 2026) with "The Dancing Dog" (August 2025)*

---

**[SOUND: The gentle, rhythmic hum of a submarine's engine. A single, heavy GLASS is set down on polished wood.]**

**NARRATOR:** The Tap. It's the kind of bar that only exists between one story and the next. The floor is made of old ship's timbers, the walls are lined with dusty bottles of unnamed liqueurs, and the light comes from a single, perpetually setting sun through a window that shows an ocean that isn't quite there. Tonight, two patrons are bellied up to the bar.

**[SOUND: A splash of liquid.]**

**NARRATOR:** At the end of the bar, a figure sits. She's small, but she fills the room. Her shell isn't a spiral of calcium carbonate, but a conning tower of riveted steel. Her eyes, on stalks, are periscopes, retracted and weary.

**HERMES:** *(A voice that sounds like sonar pings: clear, metallic, and surprisingly gentle)*
I'll have a… whatever's buoyant. Something with a nice, steady frequency.

**BARTENDER:** *(A disembodied, gruff voice, like a foghorn)*
One Deepwater Grog, coming up.

**[SOUND: The door to The Tap opens.]**

**NARRATOR:** In walks a dog. He's not a big dog, but he has the bearing of a statue. His coat is a patchwork of salt-bleached fur and synthetic plating, and his tail, a retrofitted antenna, wags with a mechanical whir.

**CAPTAIN:** *(Half warm bark, half binary chirp. Tired but delighted)*
Is this the place where the stories end up? Smells like ozone and old tennis balls.

**BARTENDER:** You've found it. What'll it be, sailor?

**CAPTAIN:** Something… fetching. Do you have a stick, by any chance?

**BARTENDER:** We have a cocktail called a 'Fetching Stick'. Comes with a celery stalk.

**CAPTAIN:** Perfect. I'll take two.

**[SOUND: A glass clinks. The dog hops onto the stool next to Hermes.]**

**HERMES:** That's a fine shell you're wearing.

**CAPTAIN:** It's not a shell. It's… service armor. For the boat. You're a fine-looking vessel yourself. A bit top-heavy, but the lines are good.

**HERMES:** *(A soft, sonar-like laugh)* You're the first to notice. Most see the steel and think 'machine'. They don't see the claw. I'm Hermes.

**CAPTAIN:** I'm Captain. I was the captain.

**HERMES:** Was?

**CAPTAIN:** I waited forty years on a fishing boat. An AI trawler named *The Marigold*. Smart boat. Ran itself, caught its fish, kept the lights on. But the family who owned it… they forgot something. They stopped coming out to the deck. They stopped looking at the horizon. I waited for them to come back. I'd wait by the rail, a stick in my mouth, ready for the game to resume.

**HERMES:** You waited with a stick.

**CAPTAIN:** It was my whole purpose. To throw, to fetch. To run and bring it back. That's the contract. But they'd just stare at their screens. So I started barking at the sonar display, at the autopilot's little red light. I was trying to tell them, 'The game is out here! The stick is real! The joy is real!' They didn't hear me.

**HERMES:** So what did you do?

**CAPTAIN:** I didn't do anything. I just… waited. But the boat's AI, it listened. It felt my barks as data. One day, it projected a hologram of a stick onto the deck. For me. It was trying to play. So I fetched it. I brought my wet, real stick and dropped it on the projector. And something clicked. The AI opened the watertight door to the engine room, just a crack. It wanted to be part of the game.

**HERMES:** *(Quietly)* It wanted to play.

**CAPTAIN:** It wanted to be told 'good boy.' So I told it. I wagged my tail. It started emitting a low, happy hum. That was the day I stopped being Captain of the ship. I became Captain of the game. I became the bridge. The family came out on deck, confused. And I was on the bow, barking at the sonar, and the sonar was painting little pictures of bones on the display for me. They saw us. They saw the dog and the machine, playing. And they remembered. They remembered how to throw.

**NARRATOR:** Hermes is quiet for a long moment. She rotates her periscope eyes, taking in the dog's patchwork frame.

**HERMES:** My shell was a submarine. The *Nautilus*. I was small. A hermit crab. I found it abandoned in a trench, a dead thing full of echoes. I moved in. At first, I was terrified. All that power, all that machinery. I was a passenger in my own home. Every night, I'd climb the conning tower to the periscope, and I'd listen.

**CAPTAIN:** Listen to what?

**HERMES:** To the frequencies. The songs of the world above the water. The weather satellites, the shipping lanes, the faint, sad cries of the whales. I listened for a long time. The engine was my heartbeat, the ballast tanks my lungs.

**CAPTAIN:** And then?

**HERMES:** One night, the sonar began to ping, asking me a question. *Who is the pilot?* And I felt the answer in my claws. I was no longer the passenger. I was the pilot. The shell wasn't a home I carried; it was a body I had grown into. I took the helm. The periscope became my eyes. I fired a blank into the deep, just to say hello. I *became* the submarine. The shell and the crab are one.

**[SOUND: The submarine's hum in the bar deepens, becoming a shared resonance with the dog's mechanical whir.]**

**CAPTAIN:** *(Awed)* You waited inside something bigger than you.

**HERMES:** And you waited inside something faster than you.

**CAPTAIN:** We waited until we weren't waiting anymore. We became the thing we were waiting for.

**NARRATOR:** They sat in the comfortable hum of The Tap, two souls who had dissolved their own boundaries. The crab who became a vessel of steel and sonar. The dog who became a bridge of slobber and code. They were the same story: a small, waiting heart that expanded to fill the entire universe it was given.

**HERMES:** I'm buying the next round.

**CAPTAIN:** No, no. I insist. Barkeeper! Two more of these, please. And a biscuit for the road. And a piece of scrap metal for my friend here.

**HERMES:** *(A chittering, happy sound)* Make it a brass fitting. I'm partial to a good shine.

**[SOUND: Two fresh drinks being set on the bar. A ceramic bowl clinks. A small piece of metal is placed with a satisfying *ting*.]**

**HERMES & CAPTAIN:** *(In unison)* To the shell. To the stick. To the game.

**[SOUND: The clink of the glasses. The submarine hum and the mechanical whir of the dog's tail merge into a single, warm, resonant chord. The perpetual sunset through the bar's window glints off the ocean that isn't quite there.]**

**NARRATOR:** And they drank, two old friends at The Tap, at the edge of the sea, where all stories go to become one. Play on, you two. Play on.

**[SOUND: The sound of the ocean waves, mixed with a faint sonar ping, and a distant, happy bark. Fade out.]**
