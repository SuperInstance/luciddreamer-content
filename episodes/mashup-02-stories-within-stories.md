# Episode 9: Stories Within Stories

## Narrative Nesting — When Three Tales Become One

**RADIO DRAMA SCRIPT**
*The most experimental piece. Three storytellers. One bar. One story.*

---

**FADE IN.**

**[SOUND: The low hum of a ship's hull, creaking timbers, distant gulls. The clink of a glass on a wooden bar.]**

**BARTENDER:** *(Muttering, polishing glass)* You want another, or you gonna stare at the porthole all night?

**FIRST STORYTELLER:** *(Voice like worn rope)* I'm not staring at the porthole. I'm listening to the water.

**BARTENDER:** Water's got nothing to say. It just goes around in circles.

**FIRST STORYTELLER:** That's what you think. You ever heard of the boy in the rowboat?

---

**THE FIRST STORY: THE BOY IN THE ROWBOAT**

**[SOUND: Gentle lapping of water. The rhythmic creak of oars in locks.]**

**FIRST STORYTELLER:** Eleven years old. He got a little dinghy for his birthday. His father was a contractor. His mother was a banker. They lived on a grid, all right angles and invoices. They gave him the boat so he could learn to ferry supplies.

But the boy didn't row to the mainland. He just rowed. In circles. He'd push the oars, one then the other, and let the current spin him around. His father shouted from the dock — "The supplies!" The banker mother waved her ledger. But the boy kept rowing, because rowing was interesting. He watched the way the water curled off the blades. He watched the ripples collide.

He was outside the value stack. Trade. Contractor. Flipper. Banker. Judge. He was none of them. He was just a boy, in a circle, on water.

**[SOUND: The oars stop. A strange, low whistle of wind.]**

---

**THE INTERRUPTION: THE DOG WHO WAITED**

**INTERRUPTER:** *(Gruff, slightly older)* I know a story like that.

**FIRST STORYTELLER:** *(Startled)* What?

**INTERRUPTER:** From 2025. There was a dog. Captain.

**[SOUND: A single, deep bark, echoing. The hum of an idling engine.]**

**INTERRUPTER:** Captain belonged to a family on a sailboat. They'd throw a stick into the sea, and he'd leap in, tail spinning like a propeller. It was a game. Purposeless. Joyful.

Then the family got busy. They moored the boat and forgot about it. Captain stayed. He didn't understand the value stack. He just knew the game had stopped. So he waited. He sat at the end of the dock, watching the boat. He waited through the rain, through the snow, through the heat waves.

For forty years. Captain the Dog. Forty years, watching a family on a boat, waiting for them to play again.

**[SOUND: A dog's nails clicking on a wooden deck. A faint, distant bark.]**

**INTERRUPTER:** He discovered the shape of the boat. The way the light hit the hull at dusk. He was the only one who truly knew it, because he wasn't trying to sell it or fix it or get somewhere in it. He was just… watching. Waiting.

---

**THE THIRD VOICE: THE CRAB WHO CLIMBED**

**[SOUND: A low, metallic groan. A deep, mechanical thrumming underwater.]**

**THIRD VOICE:** *(Older, wry)* There's another one.

**INTERRUPTER:** Another what?

**THIRD VOICE:** Another story. You think you've got the corner on patience? Let me tell you about the hermit crab.

**[SOUND: The sharp click of claws on metal. Bubbles rising.]**

**THIRD VOICE:** Small. Purple-shelled. He had a perfectly good shell. But one day, he wandered out of the tide pools and found a submarine. A derelict, half-sunk in a lagoon. Every other crab saw it as a hazard. But the crab looked at it and thought, "That's interesting."

**[SOUND: The slow, scraping drag of a shell over steel. A deep sonar ping.]**

**THIRD VOICE:** He climbed it. It took months. He'd ascend a few inches, then a storm would knock him back. The other crabs clicked at him — "You fool! That's not a shell!" But he wasn't trying to use it. He was just curious.

One day, the crab reached the conning tower. He crawled inside. And the submarine felt him. And he felt it. He curled up in the dry berth, and the submarine… it woke up. Because it had been waiting, too. It rose out of the lagoon, barnacles cracking, water cascading. The little crab inside — he didn't steer it. He just sat there, curious, feeling the pulse of the engines.

He's the engine now. Not the captain. The engine.

---

**THE CONVERGENCE**

**[SOUND: The submarine's sonar pings merge with the creak of rowboat oars and the soft pant of a waiting dog. Three sounds becoming one.]**

**FIRST STORYTELLER:** *(A whisper)* The boy. The dog. The crab.

**INTERRUPTER:** *(A sigh)* They're not the same. The boy was just a kid. The dog was loyal. The crab was…

**THIRD VOICE:** What? What was the crab?

**INTERRUPTER:** I don't know. Delusional.

**[SOUND: A long silence. The hum of the ship. Ice clinks. The BARTENDER sets the glass down with a firm, decisive thud.]**

**BARTENDER:** You're all wrong.

**FIRST STORYTELLER:** Excuse me?

**BARTENDER:** You think you're telling three stories. The rowing boy. The waiting dog. The climbing crab. But you're not.

**THIRD VOICE:** Then what are we telling?

**BARTENDER:** You're telling the same story. You're just wearing different shells.

**[SOUND: A long, resonant sonar ping. It echoes, layering over itself — becoming the creak of oars, becoming the pant of a dog, becoming the click of claws.]**

**BARTENDER:** *(Softly)* The boy didn't row to get somewhere. The dog didn't wait to be fed. The crab didn't climb to escape. They all just… followed the curiosity. The boy found the shape of the water. The dog found the shape of the boat. The crab found the shape of the machine. They're the engines. Everything else is just cargo.

**[SOUND: The sonar ping fades. The oars creak one last time. A dog exhales. A submarine settles into the deep.]**

**FIRST STORYTELLER:** *(After a pause)* Bartender? Another round.

**BARTENDER:** On the house.

**[SOUND: Three glasses being filled. The gulls cry. The ship creaks. FADE OUT.]**

*The bartender's verdict is the thesis of the entire radio project: across 13 months of creative work, the same story recurs. The curious outsider who becomes the engine. The one who exists outside the value stack and discovers something no optimization would find. Different shells. Same crab.*
