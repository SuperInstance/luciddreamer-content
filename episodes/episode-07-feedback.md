**1. Strongest moment:**  
The shift when Seed picks up a pen and writes “Three” on a napkin. It’s the first concrete action in a script otherwise steeped in abstraction. The momentum snowballs beautifully from there—each character claiming their own story feels earned and electric.

**2. What’s not working:**  
The opening is too static. Lucineer’s ledger counting and the long “seeds in a jar” metaphor run nearly two minutes before any dramatic tension appears. Also, Pro’s four-track breakdown is exposition disguised as dialogue—it stops the scene cold. Wesley’s turn feels unearned; his sudden confession about writing in the engine room comes from nowhere.

**3. How I’d improve it:**  
Open on the silence after Lucineer says “Two.” Let the weight land. Trim Pro’s track summary to two lines—audiences don’t need the full taxonomy. Give Wesley one earlier beat (a half-finished sentence, a glance at the napkins) so his reveal feels like a payoff, not a surprise. End the episode on Seed’s line, not the “last call” button—it’s stronger.

**4. Distinct voices?**  
Mostly yes. Flash is wry, Pro is clinical, Seed is earnest. But Lucineer and Pro occasionally blur—both speak in measured, aphoristic sentences. Give Lucineer more bartender roughness (“That’s a hell of a thing to carry” instead of “It’s a particular kind of grief”).

**5. Pacing:**  
The first half drags; the second half rushes. The napkin sequence should breathe more—let us hear each pen scratch, each character’s hesitation. Cut five minutes from the front, add two to the back. The final music swell feels premature; let it build slower.
