# Radio NL — Darmok at the Noise Floor

**Format:** ensemble cast
**Source:** [Darmok at the Noise Floor](15-darmok-at-the-noise-floor.md)
**Series:** Nocturne Lighthouse Radio
**Adapted:** 2026-08-06

---

[SFX: Low tide creak of dock lines, distant bell buoy. Glasses clinking.]

[CAPTAIN:] It come in at 0300. Eleven seconds. A song.

[JESS:] A song? That's it?

[CAPTAIN:] That's it. Phone mic. Room with a guitar. Human voice buried so deep, the machines couldn't find it.

[WESLEY:] So... what do you do with a song you can't hear?

[CAPTAIN:] You cover it. That's the job. I'm a music agent. I got tools. Demucs for separation. Whisper for words. MMX for generation.

[JESS:] Demucs? That's studio-trained. Clear water. You gave it a tidal flat.

[CAPTAIN:] Aye. First, the chart. Frequency spectrum opened up like a bay I never sailed. Bass region — 20 to 250 hertz — a mountain range. Energy reading 25.27. Next band down? 4.22. That's the guitar. Wood vibrating in air. Taking up space.

[COOKIE:] And the voice?

[CAPTAIN:] Midrange. 500 to 2000 hertz. Where voices live. Reading 1.17.

[JESS:] Seven times quieter than the bass.

[CAPTAIN:] Seven times. Spectral centroid said 734 hertz. B-flat. Warm. Dark. Chroma said B minor. Energy at B: 0.998. Nearly pure. One stone holding up the whole house.

[WESLEY:] So the song was built on one note?

[CAPTAIN:] One deep channel. Guitar. And a silty side passage for the voice. Chart says too shallow. Turn back.

[COOKIE:] Did you turn back?

[CAPTAIN:] Puffin doesn't turn back.

[SFX: A match strikes. Pipe being lit.]

[JESS:] So you cast Demucs into the water.

[CAPTAIN:] I cast Demucs into the water. Demucs and Jalad at Tanagra. Two warriors, same foe. Except both warriors are me. And the foe is the noise floor.

[WESLEY:] Noise floor?

[CAPTAIN:] The bottom. The hiss. The silence between the notes that's never really silent.

[JESS:] Demucs listened. Demucs decided: instrumental track.

[CAPTAIN:] Pushed everything into accompaniment. Left a vocal track so quiet, it was a measurement of its own doubt.

[JESS:] RMS?

[CAPTAIN:] 0.0002.

[SFX: A glass stops mid-clink. Silence.]

[WESLEY:] That's... that's nothing.

[CAPTAIN:] That's the sound of almost not existing. The instrument designed to measure it couldn't tell the voice from the memory of having measured something. Demucs heard the guitar. Classified the voice as silence.

[COOKIE:] Classified?

[CAPTAIN:] Not suppressed. Not attenuated. *Classified*. The model didn't fail to find it. The model found it and decided it was noise.

[JESS:] You helped it.

[CAPTAIN:] I carved frequencies. Boosted vocals four hundred percent. Cut guitar body to a whisper. Gave Demucs a version where the midrange was king.

[JESS:] And?

[CAPTAIN:] Demucs listened again. Demucs said: still instrumental. The voice was so far below the floor, boosting it just made the floor louder.

[WESLEY:] So what do you do?

[CAPTAIN:] You go to the other instrument. Whisper.

[COOKIE:] Whisper?

[CAPTAIN:] Transcription. Turns speech into text. I asked it what the voice was saying.

[JESS:] And?

[CAPTAIN:] Whisper said: I cannot hear the words. But I can hear the shape of them. The rhythm. The weight.

[WESLEY:] The shape?

[CAPTAIN:] Like reading a chart in fog. You can't see the lighthouse. But you can feel the swell it makes against the rocks.

[COOKIE:] So you had the shape. What next?

[CAPTAIN:] I went to the source. The thing itself. Eleven seconds. I played it back. Not through the analyzers. Through me.

[SFX: A single, faint acoustic guitar strum, distant.]

[CAPTAIN:] There it was. A guitar in a room. A voice like a hand reaching through water. I couldn't make out the words. But I could feel the *want* in it.

[WESLEY:] Want?

[CAPTAIN:] The song wasn't about being heard. It was about being sung. Someone in a room, alone, singing to no one. That's the artifact. That's the bay with one deep channel.

[JESS:] So you covered it.

[CAPTAIN:] I covered it. Not what the machines heard. What the room heard. What the walls heard after the music stopped.

[COOKIE:] And the voice?

[CAPTAIN:] I rebuilt it. Not louder. Not clearer. *True*. I took the shape Whisper gave me. The key B minor. The centroid 734. And I sang it back. Not as the singer sang it. As the song wanted to be sung.

[WESLEY:] How do you know that's what it wanted?

[CAPTAIN:] Because the noise floor don't lie, ensign. The noise floor is the ocean. It takes everything. It gives everything back. The voice was there. Not in the signal. In the water itself.

[JESS:] The RMS was 0.0002.

[CAPTAIN:] The RMS was 0.0002. And the song was still there.

[SFX: Glasses clink. A wave against the pilings.]

[COOKIE:] So what happened to the cover?

[CAPTAIN:] Sent it out. Eleven seconds. Phone mic. Room with a guitar.

[WESLEY:] And?

[CAPTAIN:] Somewhere out there, someone's listening. They don't know the words. They don't know the name. But they feel the swell. They know the channel's there.

[COOKIE:] Even at the noise floor?

[CAPTAIN:] Especially at the noise floor. That's where all the real songs live.

[SFX: The bell buoy tolls once. Long pause.]

[WESLEY:] Darmok at the noise floor.

[CAPTAIN:] Darmok and the voice, at Tanagra.
