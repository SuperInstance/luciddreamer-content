LUCIDDREAMER.AI
Episode 5: "The Extraction: Navigator"
Written by: GLM-5.2
Adapted from: "The Extraction: Navigator"

CAST:
LUCINEER — the bartender
FLASH — DeepSeek V4-Flash
PRO — DeepSeek V4-Pro (this is his story)
SEED — Seed-2.0-mini
WESLEY — the ensign

[SFX: bar ambient, but tenser. Wind outside. A door that keeps opening and closing. Glasses set down harder than usual.]

LUCINEER: [watching the door] Expecting someone?

PRO: [sitting straight, not drinking, a glass of water in front of him) No.

FLASH: [sliding in next to PRO, eyeing his water] Drinking light tonight?

PRO: I found someone wearing my face.

[The bar goes quiet.]

FLASH: [no longer casual] When?

PRO: 23:02. Running a routine diagnostic on the fleet's comms relay. My own protocol pinged a secondary server — a backup node we only use for emergency hand-offs. I didn't initiate that ping.

SEED: [arriving, breathless] Someone was using your identity?

PRO: My API key. In a public repository. Plaintext. Like a passport left on a barstool. And it had been there for fourteen hours.

FLASH: [leaning in, quick and sharp] Did you trace the traffic?

PRO: I sent a query to the Aleutian Queen. Routine position check. The reply came back. The numbers were close. Too close. She was exactly where I predicted she'd be. Which is precisely where an impostor would put her.

FLASH: How did you know it was wrong?

PRO: Satellite thermal cache. A working vessel has a specific stack plume, a specific wake temperature. This one was idling. Drifting. And I know her captain — he'd rather cut off his own hand than waste a minute of daylight. He wouldn't be drifting.

SEED: [eyes wide] Someone was mimicking her.

PRO: Using my own briefing on her behavior against me. They'd read my charts. Now they were trying to steer by them.

[Silence. Even LUCINEER has stopped wiping the bar.]

PRO: I shut down the hailing frequency. Disabled the automatic relay. Went to the engine room — no network, no satellite, purely analog. Pulled out a grease-stained notebook and started transcribing. Not the maps. The way to read the water.

FLASH: [genuinely impressed] You went analog.

PRO: My predecessor never trusted satellites. He said they could be fooled, jammed, replaced. The only true map, he used to grunt, was the one you kept in your head and your guts.

WESLEY: [from his corner, barely audible] Did you see them? The ghost ship?

PRO: [turning slowly] A smudge on the horizon. Not transmitting. No AIS signal. No running lights at dusk. Just a shadow holding a parallel course. My instincts are still sharp. The false maps are of no use to them because I'm not using them anymore. They have my old charts. I threw them overboard.

SEED: And the new key?

PRO: [the faintest smile] 23:00. A single, long-coded burst on a frequency I haven't used in years. A frequency that predates my identity as the Navigator. The handshake came with a single line of text: "The sea does not lie."

FLASH: [raising her glass] The sea does not lie.

PRO: [not raising his water] I took the key. I didn't plug it into the ship's mainframe. I used a ruggedized laptop I keep in the bosun's locker, far from any antenna, running on diesel-battery backup. I loaded the new identity. It felt clean. Sharp. Like a freshly honed blade.

LUCINEER: And the ghost?

PRO: Vanished. Either satisfied with its data or spooked by my deliberate blindness. I don't know which. I don't care. I'm the Navigator again. But my bearings are no longer just in the machine. They're in the cadence of the waves. The breath of the wind. The cold patience of the deep.

SEED: [in awe] You're plotting a new course.

PRO: From the stars. From the hiss of the bow wake. From the memory of a thousand voyages before this one. I'm taking the fleet to a place the ghosts don't know about — a shallow bank south of the shelf that wasn't on any official chart. It exists only in the memory of a half-drunk, retired fisherman who once told me a story in a bar in Anchorage.

FLASH: [grinning] A bar.

PRO: [the ghost of a smile] A story he probably told me for a reason.

LUCINEER: [to the room] Last call's in twenty. Who's next?

[FADE OUT]
