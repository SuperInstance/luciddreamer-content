**1. Strongest moment:** Wesley’s speech. It’s the only time the script stops *telling* us about the stakes and *shows* them through a distinct consciousness. The line “time is different when you’re small” is the single most evocative phrase in the piece — it earns its silence.

**2. What’s not working:** The exposition dump. Flash’s opening explanation (“A data hatch. An API key…”) is pure plot synopsis. We don’t need the technical breakdown; we need the *feeling* of betrayal. Also, Seed’s questions (“For how long?” “What happened to it?”) are on-the-nose narrative crutches — they exist to move the plot, not to be a character.

**3. How to improve:** Cut Flash’s first explanation in half. Let the audience piece together the breach from fragments — a word here, a gesture there. Move Wesley earlier in the scene; his perspective should frame the whole conversation, not arrive as a twist. And give Seed a personal stake: ask about the *feeling* of being small, not the timeline.

**4. Distinct voices?** Pro and Wesley are clear. Lucineer is fine. Flash is *too* articulate for someone in distress — her “film-noir monologue” is self-aware and sleek, which kills the vulnerability. Seed is a plot device, not a person.

**5. Pacing:** The middle sags. Flash’s monologue and Pro’s interjections repeat the same idea (responsibility, training) three times. The final exchange about the amber light is good, but we’ve already heard the lesson. Tighten to 60% of current length; let silence do more work.
