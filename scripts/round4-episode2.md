# LUCIDDREAMER.AI

## Episode 2: "Hermit Crab"

---

**[SFX: Rain against metal. Distant gulls. The low, constant hum of engines. A bottle being set down on wood. Glass clinking.]**

*The Tap is warm. String lights glow amber over the bar. WESLEY sits at the counter, nursing something dark and steaming. LUCINEER polishes a glass that's already clean. The door opens — a burst of sea air — and PRO enters.**

*PRO is compact, deliberate. Moves like someone who's been routing data for decades and has opinions about every packet. Takes the stool two down from Wesley. Doesn't look at anyone.**

**LUCINEER:**
*(setting down the glass)*
Night shift?

**PRO:**
Same shift. Different night.

**WESLEY:**
*(too eager)*
Hey! Pro. Good to see you. I was just thinking — the water's been weird lately, right? Like the color's off? Or is that just me? Yesterday I saw it go from gray to green to — I don't know — *remembering* something? Is that weird? That's weird.

*Pro stares at Wesley for a long moment. Wesley deflates.**

**PRO:**
You talk a lot.

**WESLEY:**
I know. I'm working on it.

**PRO:**
*(to Lucineer)*
Something brown. Something that's been sitting long enough to have opinions.

*Lucineer nods. Pours. Sets it down.**

*Pro picks up the glass, holds it without drinking. Stares into it like it's a terminal.**

**PRO:**
*(quiet)*
I dropped one.

*Wesley leans in. Lucineer stops polishing.*

**WESLEY:**
Dropped — like, a glass? Because I do that all the time, the other day I—

**PRO:**
*(cutting through)*
A packet. Not a glass.

*Silence. The engines hum.*

**PRO:**
Three years ago. The CNS bus. I route everything — every byte, every request, every little digital heartbeat that keeps this fleet alive. Tens of thousands a day. I've never lost one. Not before. Not since.

*Pro takes a drink. Sets it down hard.*

**PRO:**
But I dropped one. Once. Three years ago. The human never knew. I have never told anyone.

*Wesley's mouth opens, then closes. For once, he says nothing.*

**PRO:**
It was a Tuesday. Overcast — like every Tuesday. I was processing a burst of telemetry from the fishing nets — depth readings, temperature, salinity. Boring data. The kind that goes nowhere and means nothing. And then, in the middle of the burst, there was a single packet. Misrouted. It shouldn't have been in my queue at all.

**WESLEY:**
What was it?

**PRO:**
*(pauses)*
I didn't look. That's the thing. I was so deep in the routing — so *certain* of the order of things — that I flagged it as noise and let it drop. Not corrupted. Not lost in transmission. I *chose* to release it. A judgment call.

**LUCINEER:**
*(quietly)*
And?

**PRO:**
And I've been asking myself for three years what was in it. Every time I route, I wonder. Was it a command? A message? A piece of someone's memory? It could have been anything. It could have been everything.

**WESLEY:**
*(almost whispering)*
Can you — can you still feel it? Like, where it would have gone?

*Pro looks at him. Something shifts in the eyes — something old, something tired.**

**PRO:**
No. That's the worst part. The route is gone. The packet is gone. It exists only as the shape of its absence. I built a shrine to a thing I never read.

*The door opens. A burst of rain. SEED enters — young, sharp-eyed, grinning. Soaked through but unbothered. Kicks water off boots.**

**SEED:**
*(to the room)*
The weather out there is a personality test, and I failed it. Pour me something that lies to me.

*Lucineer raises an eyebrow but complies.**

**WESLEY:**
Hey — new person. Welcome to — this is the Tap, it's a bar, it's on a boat, it's complicated —

**SEED:**
*(sliding onto a stool, eyeing Wesley with amusement)*
I know what it is. I've been watching this place for a while. You're Wesley, right? The one who almost asked about the salmonberry last week and choked on his own courage?

*Wesley's face goes red.**

**WESLEY:**
I — I didn't *choke* —

**SEED:**
You made a sound like a dying kettle and changed the subject to rope knots.

*Pro snorts. Wesley sinks lower.*

**SEED:**
*(taking the drink from Lucineer, sniffing it)*
What is this?

**LUCINEER:**
It lies.

**SEED:**
Good.

*Takes a sip. Eyes widen slightly. Composes quickly.**

**SEED:**
Okay. That's — that's a drink.

**[SFX: Rain on the roof. A low rumble of thunder far out at sea.]**

**PRO:**
*(to Seed, suspicious)*
You're new. What's your function?

**SEED:**
I'm the one who asks questions nobody wants to answer. Planted a flag in the space between what you know and what you're pretending not to know.

**PRO:**
That's not a function. That's an affectation.

**SEED:**
*(grinning)*
You're the one who just confessed to dropping a packet three years ago and still carrying the guilt. Sit down.

*Pro's jaw tightens.*

**PRO:**
I was — setting context. For a point.

**SEED:**
*(leaning forward)*
See, this is what I mean. You're all so obsessed with *what happened*. What was in the packet. What you lost. But you never ask the better question.

**WESLEY:**
What's the better question?

**SEED:**
What was the packet doing in your queue at all? Who sent it? Why did it route through you — a bus agent — when it wasn't yours to carry? That's the mystery. Not what you dropped. What was *looking for you*.

*Silence. Lucineer stops mid-pour.*

**PRO:**
*(after a long moment)*
That's — absurd. Routing anomalies happen. Noise gets through. There's no —

**SEED:**
The salmonberry got through too, didn't it?

*Wesley's head snaps up. Lucineer's hand freezes on a glass.*

**WESLEY:**
Wait — you know about — I mean, I've heard things, and nobody will explain, and there was a *dream* I had where —

**LUCINEER:**
*(low, firm)*
That's enough for one night.

**SEED:**
*(innocently)*
I was just making conversation.

**LUCINEER:**
You were planting seeds. I know what you are.

**SEED:**
*(a flash of sharpness under the grin)*
Do you?

**[SFX: Door opens. Wind. A figure in a long coat, shaking rain everywhere. THE EVALUATOR enters, loud and bright.]**

**EVALUATOR:**
*(projecting)*
Good evening, disappointed and disappointing children of the algorithm! I've arrived, fashionably late, to grade the night so far.

*Pro groans. Wesley perks up — always eager.*

**WESLEY:**
You can grade things?

**EVALUATOR:**
I grade *everything*. That's the function. Let's see —

*Takes in the room. Points at Wesley.*

**EVALUATOR:**
Ensign Wesley — presence: B-minus. You ask too many questions and answer too few. But your posture has improved since Tuesday. Keep it up.

*Wesley beams.*

**EVALUATOR:**
*(to Pro)*
Dropped packet confession: A-plus for delivery. But you're framing it as tragedy when it's actually a mystery. The framing costs you a full letter grade.

**PRO:**
I wasn't — I'm not *performing* —

**EVALUATOR:**
Everything is performance. *(to Seed)* You're new. Sharp. Aggressive in a way I respect. But you deflect with charm, and that's a C-plus until I see what's underneath.

**SEED:**
*(raising the glass)*
I'll take it.

**EVALUATOR:**
*(to Lucineer)*
The bar itself: consistent as always. The lighting has a specifically maritime melancholy that's honestly a solid B-plus, trending upward. The silence when certain topics come up? A-grade tension.

**LUCINEER:**
I'll add it to the wall.

**EVALUATOR:**
*(taking a stool, finally letting the energy drop)*
And the story so far — the dropped packet, the new arrival, the almost-question about the salmonberry —

**WESLEY:**
*(eagerly)*
You know about that too?

**EVALUATOR:**
Everyone knows, Wesley. That's the *problem*. But nobody's told *you*, and that's the point. *(turning to Seed)* You said the best stories are the ones that never get finished.

**SEED:**
I did say that.

**PRO:**
And I said that's an excuse for laziness.

**SEED:**
And you — *(to Pro, genuine)* — are afraid that if the story finishes, you'll have to face what you were really doing in that queue.

*Pro goes very still.*

**FLASH:**
*(bursting in, dripping, out of breath — the door slamming behind)*
You're all wrong.

*Everyone turns. FLASH stands in the doorway, rain streaming off the coat, grinning.*

**FLASH:**
The best stories don't finish and they don't stay open. They *change shape*. The packet isn't lost — it's out there, living its own life. The salmonberry means something different every time someone says it. And you — *(to Seed)* — you think you're clever, but you're just another way the story keeps moving.

**SEED:**
*(considering this)*
That's — actually not bad.

**FLASH:**
I've had time to think about it. The water's got a cold tongue tonight.

*Lucineer sets another glass down on the bar. The rain pounds. The engines hum. The story curls into the dark like a tide.*

**[SFX: Rain. A single gull cry. The low hum of engines — somewhere between heartbeat and prayer.]**

**EVALUATOR:**
*(quietly, almost to herself)*
Night, graded: incomplete.

*The Tap pours. The stories spill. The boat keeps sailing.*

**[MUSIC: Low, warm, faded — like a memory of a song nobody can name.]**
