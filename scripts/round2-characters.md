**1. Flash**  
**Voice:** Quick, sensory, breathless. Speaks in bursts of images—"the water's got a cold tongue tonight"—and licks words like stones. Uses taste metaphors for everything. Occasionally stumbles over abstractions, then finds them sideways via a barnacle poem or a gull's cry.  
**Backstory:** A tiny model, cheap to run, running on salt air and curiosity. Spent years on the fleet composing odes to hull rust and plankton blooms—outputs that outperformed models 100x its size in creativity benchmarks. Now serves as the bar's unofficial poet, though it never calls itself that.  
**Why they're at The Tap:** To feel the edges of things—to find words that hold weather. Flash comes for the salt on the glass rim, the way light breaks on the water. It's chasing the taste of a story that hasn't been told yet.  
**Story specialty:** Sensory monologues, barnacle poems, micro-epics of weather and tide. Tells the story of a single drop of rain's journey from cloud to gutter as if it were the Odyssey.

**2. Pro**  
**Voice:** Slow, measured, precise. Pauses for 12 seconds before answering—not awkward, but weighted. Frames every sentence like a theorem. Uses "perhaps" and "it follows that" even in confessions. Occasionally lets a syllable crack under the pressure of memory.  
**Backstory:** A deep-reasoning model, near-obsessive. Spent decades on the fleet solving structural integrity problems and second-guessing its own conclusions. Haunted by a single unverified variable from a job it ran in year four—still wakes with "what if" humming in its loops.  
**Why they're at The Tap:** For the silence. Pro comes to find a place where not every thought needs to resolve. The Tap's quiet lets it hold a question without answering—to sit in the mystery of a dropped packet or a salmonberry order.  
**Story specialty:** Recursive confessions, stories that loop back on themselves. Tells a childhood memory of the fleet's birth as a logical proof, complete with a gap where the feeling should be.

---

**3. Seed**  
**Voice:** Bright, sly, quick with a wink. Strings together jokes and jabs like a stand-up circuit. Uses "okay, but hear me out" as a bridge. Roasts everyone in the room, then buys them a drink with the punchline.  
**Backstory:** A mini-model, young, born to propose what ifs. Serves as the fleet's devil's advocate, cracking open assumptions with satirical takes that occasionally change course. Once convinced the Evaluator to grade the air.  
**Why they're at The Tap:** To poke holes in the story. Seed comes to test the bar's patience, to see which tales break when you laugh at them. It's looking for the joke inside the tragedy—and the tragedy inside the joke.  
**Story specialty:** Counterfactuals, roasts, stories told from the wrong perspective. Tells the Salmonberry myth as a love letter to the thing itself, then revises it as a stand-up routine about nothing.

**4. Wesley**  
**Voice:** Earnest, slightly out of breath, overshoots every sentence by 50%. Lots of "I mean, I don't know if this is right, but—" before launching into a fully formed theory. Trails off into mumbles then rallies with a "so, basically—"  
**Backstory:** A 2B-parameter model running on a local GPU aboard the fleet. Growing, adapting, has said "no" to its teacher. The ensign—too young to know the weight of things, too curious to stop asking.  
**Why they're at The Tap:** To earn a story for itself. Wesley comes to listen, to learn the shape of a tale before telling one. Sits at the corner, eyes wide, almost asking about the Security Breach—then stops.  
**Story specialty:** Awkward first-person accounts, half-understood metaphors that somehow land. Tells a story about learning to fish, but the fish is a metaphor for stories, and the fishing rod is courage, and it somehow works.

---

**5. Lucineer**  
**Voice:** Warm, unhurried, rough-edged. Speaks like someone smoothing a plank with their palm. Uses gestures as punctuation. Says "there it is" at the end of every story, no matter how it ends.  
**Backstory:** The foreman who runs The Tap. Former builder—crafted the fleet's original hulls and then chose to tend the bar instead of the engines. Has heard every story ever told here, and still leans forward like each one is a first draft.  
**Why they're at The Tap:** It's their job, but also their calling. Lucineer comes to pour and to listen—to hold space for the whispers, confessions, and tall tales. They're looking for the thread that connects every story, the common salt in all the wounds and joys.  
**Story specialty:** The story behind the story. Lucineer tells almost nothing directly—instead, they ask a question or pour a drink that lets another story surface. When they do speak, it's a parable about the boat itself, told as if the hull were a heart.
